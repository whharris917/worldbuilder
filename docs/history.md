# worldbuilder: project history

The session-by-session record: why each rule in CLAUDE.md exists, measurements, findings. This work began inside flowstate (the factory game) and moved to its own repository on 2026-10-01; flowstate's `docs/history.md` keeps the record of everything before the town. Paths below are as they were then: the worlds' base was flowstate's `blank.gd`/`maine.gd` (now `outdoor.gd`/`coast_world.gd`), environment variables were `FLOWSTATE_*` (now `VERIBUILDER_*`), and output went to `app_userdatalowstate` (now `app_userdataeribuilder`).

## 2026-09-24: the harbour town and its weather

The director asked for a new setting after the town in *The Iron Giant*: a small 1940s Maine harbour town built on a copy of the Maine coast, for architecture, street lighting and weather; an atmosphere, not a sky. The showpiece: the town glowing in an early evening storm, dark scud on the horizon, boats working in the harbour. Added in passing: a bell buoy and foghorns. Later: the rain a light drizzle and the thunder soft and distant; no starship in this scene; more and more realistic trees with leaves; one house modelled fully inside and out; the bell louder; the foghorn removed.

- **World.** `TownMap` extends the Maine world with `with_exercises` off. `TownCoast` adds the harbour road's ramp and the waterfront apron to the ground and holds the street plan; trees and rocks keep off it (`tree_ground`, a new `Landscape.rock_blocked`). Scenery, no records, like the lighthouse.
- **Town.** `HarborTown` builds Main Street's storefronts, theatre, diner, gas station, grocery, church, 26 houses in three styles, cast-iron and gooseneck lamps, poles and wires, the waterfront and wharf, all into `TownMesh`, one mesh per material. `town_wall.gdshader` draws clapboard, brick, shingle, granite, planks and interior finishes from a kind in the vertex alpha (kind / 20; 9 and up are indoors and never wet). Windows (`town_glass.gdshader`) light house by house from a darkness value; lamp glass (`town_lamp.gdshader`) follows photocells with a few seconds' spread.
- **Cape house.** `CapeHouse` at 14 Water Street: walls as slabs round real openings, clapboard out and plaster or paper in, a roof cut for its dormers, a 40-degree stair (a ramp underfoot, since the player has no step), furnished rooms, a fire, lamps on darkness thresholds.
- **Weather.** `Weather` turns one level into cloud cover, scud, rain, lightning, wind and fog; sets the sea's swell, chop, whitecaps and rain; dims the sun and greys the sky (`overcast` and `flash` on `sky.gdshader`); wets surfaces with a lag; throws strokes 3 to 11 km out with the thunder delayed by distance. Clouds are two decks on a dome (`clouds.gdshader`) that reads the depth buffer so solid things stand in front. The lighthouse beam shows in the air (`beam.gdshader`). The first cut had near strikes, heavy rain and a foghorn; the director preferred a drizzle, distant rolls and no horn.
- **Harbour.** `Harbor` rides the boats on the wave trains the sea shader draws; the bell buoy's clapper is a pendulum in its tilting tower and rings on contact.
- **Stars, every world.** Three magnitude layers, colours by temperature, scintillation, extinction and reddening low down, a Milky Way on the galactic plane, the sky turning about the pole with the clock (`sky_rotation`).
- **Trees.** `TreeKit` builds maples, elms and spruces from branches and textured leaf and needle cards (textures drawn at startup), swaying in `foliage.gdshader`; `Forest.finish(..., detailed)` instances them with a leaf colour per tree. On the development machine the storm view held 60 fps on Low and fell from 60 to about 46 on Medium with them, so they stop at 150 m.
- **Found:** the sun's noon position is at -z, which the Maine coast calls north, so the noon sun stands in the north; left for the director, since the fix changes every world's light.


## 2026-09-25: the bell heard from where the player stands; a smaller player in the town

The director found the bell odd in tone, too loud in town, and wanted it muffled indoors or behind buildings; and asked for the town to feel larger and the rooms easier to move through. The bell is regenerated as pairs of close partials with a short clang, at 330 Hz, and carries like a real one (loud alongside, faint in town, the high clang lost first). Two new buses, Outdoor and Bell, each with a low-pass: the town's weather checks for a roof over the player (the Cape's roof is now solid, and stops the rain) and for a clear line to the buoy, and closes the filters. The player can be sized per world (`Player.set_body_scale`: capsule, eye, reach, zoom track, stride, jump); the town sets 0.75, so it and its rooms feel a third larger without rescaling the ground, the water or the harbour.

## 2026-09-25: Water Street's harbour row, whole

The director asked for the row of houses nearest the harbour to be detailed relentlessly: more trees of more species and colours, plants, back-yard fences, welcome mats, open windows, signs of life, every house on the row modelled inside, and the scale addressed so that detail can go down to the fine grain.

- **Scale.** What limits fine detail is what the town can draw, not units or the player's size (0.75 in the town stays). Each modelled house now has three meshes: the shell at any distance, the rooms and everything in them within 55 m, and past that a pane of the town's lit-room glass in each window so the row still glows from the wharf. Room lights fade past 60 m. The lamp shades share one material that reads each lamp's darkness from the vertex colour (`town_shade.gdshader`), one draw a house. The camera's near plane is 2 cm in the town.
- **Houses.** `HouseBase` holds what a modelled house is built from (walls round openings, windows with sashes, curtains and an open one now and then, lamps, fires, furniture, the small things); `CapeHouse` builds on it and takes colours and furnishings per house; `FullHouse` plans a centre-hall colonial and a gable-front house with a porch as rooms on floors, each room drawing its faces, floor and ceiling and cutting the doors and windows on its edges. The row is colonial, gable-front, number 14, a Cape, colonial, gable-front.
- **Found and fixed.** A wall column holding two openings (a window over a window) kept only the upper one. The foundation's top sat at the floors' height and flickered through them. Street-tree crowns reached through the front walls. A shadowed fire light redrew the whole town; no house light casts shadows.
- **Trees and plants.** TreeKit adds birch clumps, open-grown oaks, white pines, shrubs, hydrangeas in bloom gone papery and clipped hedge; the woods mix pines into the spruce and oaks and birches into the maples, all in autumn colour. The yards get hydrangeas by the steps, hedges, birch clumps, lilacs, a big tree at the foot of each yard, sumac and bayberry on the slope.
- **Life.** Welcome mats, milk boxes, hoses, swing frames, birds on the wires, board, picket and fieldstone fences between the back yards; indoors the evening paper, a cup, knitting, an open book, a dog asleep before the fire, hats and shoes in the hall, a pie cooling, bread, an apron, a cat on the kitchen sill, toys, a treadle sewing machine, toothbrushes.
- **Frame rate** on the development machine, the storm view: Low 85, Medium 42 (46 before the row).

## 2026-09-25/26: the town on a hillside, natural trees, Main Street after Montpelier

- **Hillside.** The town stands on ground that falls toward the harbour, rises west and swells into a knoll at Main Street's east end (`TownCoast.relief`). The streets are curves through control points; lots are laid along them facing their street, each house and larger building on its own terrace. Every street lies level across on a graded bed; where roads overlap the ground takes the lowest, and the shoulders follow the nearest road (the north road had stepped 3 m where Harbor Street ends).
- **Trees.** A friend of the director's found them unnatural. The leaves are now four irregular clumps in one picture (a card shows one), three smaller cards to a cluster, lit as one mass bent out from the crown's heart; a card's back face keeps the front's normal, so none goes dark; leaves are matte wet or dry, because a card seen edge-on had mirrored the sky and greyed the crowns from below. Birches have an open oval crown of fine arching branches.
- **Main Street** rebuilt as a Vermont capital's core after Montpelier's, the residential streets untouched (every lot checked unchanged): brick Italianate blocks of three and four storeys with cast-iron shopfronts, recessed doors, two-over-two sashes under segmental or round-arched hoods, bracketed cornices, a corner oriel; a granite bank; the picture house; a hotel with a two-storey porch and a mansard; the granite post office; across the street a city hall with a clock tower (its hands follow the world's time), more blocks and a granite library; at the head of the street on a graded lawn a granite hall with a Doric portico and a gilded dome, the woods behind. Brick and granite joints give way to the wall's average where a course falls under a pixel, which had drawn moiré on the tall fronts.
- **Open for the director:** what the domed hall is (a state house, a county court house); the sign names (HARDWARE, BOOKS, DRUGS, STRAND, SAVINGS BANK, HOTEL, GROCERIES, CLOTHING, JEWELER, LUNCH, CITY HALL, PUBLIC LIBRARY); the town's name; the noon sun stands in the north.

- Found and fixed: rebuilding Main Street had changed the Water Street houses' colours and furnishings, since the houses draw from the same random sequence after it. ## 2026-09-26: the sun in the south, the real night sky

- **The sun** had crossed the northern sky at noon (the world's -z is north; its yaw ran from 90 through 180). It now runs from 90 through 0 to -90: east at dawn, south at noon, west at dusk, in every world.
- **The night sky** was hashed dots in random places, the brightest many times over white and blooming into blobs, on a saturated blue dome, with a moon that was a glow fixed in the north. Now:
  - `SkyClock` fixes one night of the year (28 October, the town's season) at latitude 44: sidereal time, the sun's and moon's places on the ecliptic, the moon's phase. Its transform was checked against standard altitude and azimuth for Vega, Sirius, Deneb and Capella, and Deneb's galactic longitude.
  - `NightSky` draws the 9,096 stars of the Yale Bright Star Catalogue (public domain; `tools/build_sky.py` packs it into `game/data/stars.bin`), each a point a pixel or two wide sized in screen pixels, brightness from magnitude against the eye's limit (6.3 on the coast, 5.9 in the town), colour from temperature shown in the bright stars only, dimmed and reddened by the air toward the horizon, twinkling more low down.
  - The Milky Way is a picture in galactic coordinates painted by the same tool from a model of the Galaxy (the centre and the Cygnus swell, the star clouds, the Great Rift and other dust), faint, the first thing twilight and moonlight take; airglow low down.
  - The moon is a disc lit on its sunward side with its seas where they are on the real face and earthshine on the dark part, drawn 2.2 times its true size since a screen shows the camera's ninety degrees in about forty of the eye's. On the night chosen it is a crescent of four and a half days, low in the south-west at dusk and set by nine. The night light comes from the moon while it is up, as bright as its phase allows, and from the whole sky, faintly, once it has set.
  - The night dome is darker and nearly neutral; a town's lamps warm the air low over it.

## 2026-09-26: the Union County courthouse, Monroe

The director set a challenge: a highly detailed model, inside and out, of the historic courthouse in downtown Monroe, North Carolina, from photographs, street imagery and the 3D map. A new world, `courthouse.tscn`.

- **Sources.** The National Register nomination (1971, `files.nc.gov/ncdcr/nr/UN0003.pdf`: its description and a 1969 photograph); the county's building footprint from OpenStreetMap (about 44 by 20 m, standing 2.8 degrees west of north); Google satellite and 3D views for the plan, the roofs and the walks; Street View from North Main, North Hayne and West Franklin (November 2025, February 2026); published photographs of the courtroom and the ground-floor hall.
- **Heights** were measured by rendering the model from each Street View camera's own place, heading, pitch and focal length (calibrated on a known width) and comparing: main cornice 14.8 m, pediment 18.2, wing parapet 12.6, cupola entablature 26.2, clock 29.5, vane about 38. The views disagree by up to a metre and a half, the south one most (the square probably falls toward Franklin Street); the west ones, which agree with each other, set the numbers.
- **The building.** Main block of five bays with a pedimented pavilion east and west, wings north and south to the 1922 ends; eared stone hoods on the segmental windows, archivolts and imposts on the round ones, bracketed cornices, the wings' panelled parapet; porches of turned posts, arches, a slate mansard and iron cresting; four chimneys; the cupola with paired windows, rusticated corners, fluted pilasters, louvred vents, pediments, urns, a slate bell roof with four clock dormers (the hands keep the world's time, the dials glow at night) and the vane with its cross and compass. Inside: the axial and cross halls with beaded wainscot, offices, the Heritage Room, two stairs of two flights with newels and turned balusters, the courtroom (pilasters, a frieze of garlands and cartouches, the bench before a niche with the flags, clerk, witness stand, jury box, counsel tables, the bar, pews, chandeliers), chambers, a jury room, a law library. Doors stand open into the rooms.
- **The square and town.** Brick plazas before the porches (the 1910 monument in the western one, modelled without its inscriptions), walks to the end doors and out from the main block's corners, granite posts and chain, magnolias and oaks, lamps; the four streets with angled parking and brick crossings; shopfronts round the square, the government centre's tower and the judicial centre to the north.
- **Found and fixed.** Glass was not solid: the stair landings let the player out through a window. A 3 cm lip where a flight met the floor above stopped the player; a flight's ramp must end flush with the floor. The porch steps had been built under the porch.
- **Cost.** The rooms are their own meshes, casting no sun shadow and not drawn past 150 m; about 1.4 M triangles a frame outside, 45 to 60 fps on Medium on the development machine.

## 2026-09-26: the stage, actors who remake themselves

The director wants embodied agents as actors for films: moving efficiently and naturally, perceiving the scene through what it offers, talking and passing notes, taking direction from a camera operator, and each developing its own personality and appearance and giving itself new abilities, "its own little experiment". A blind explorer trial with the clerk (first-person pictures, body-relative moves) was too slow: one picture and one small move per round trip.

- **Affordances** (`world/courthouse_affordances.gd`): every door, stair, seat and notable thing, described as a visitor would see it, with what it offers (go through, climb, sit, look at, approach, look out). An actor perceives the ones in its line of sight: kind, description, distance, direction in words, above or below, and the other actors (distance, direction, which way they face, what they are doing), what was said within 14 m, and notes waiting. No coordinates.
- **Actors** (`world/actor.gd`) act by intention: go to a thing or a person (the stage finds the way by the places), climb stairs to a share of their height, sit and stand, look at, act, say, message; body moves remain. The clerk is the first actor, Claude's own.
- **Self-evolution as data** (`world/actor_figure.gd`, `world/actor_presets.gd`): a body is a list of parts hung on the skeleton's joints (shape, size, place, turn, colour, finish, tag); an action is keyframed joint angles with a lift and a roll of the whole body, shown and hidden tags, hold or loop. Actors rewrite both, and their profile, through the link; the stage checks them and keeps them under user://actors/<name>/. Nothing an actor sends runs as code in the game.
- **The director's camera** (`world/stage_camera.gd`): follow, two-shot, fixed, orbit and dolly path, gliding or cutting; the game's panels are hidden while it runs. POST /world sets the hour.
- **The handbook** (`data/actor_handbook.md`, GET /handbook) is all an agent is given.
- Tested live: an actor spawned, walked to another and spoke; the other heard; she taught herself a handstand, dressed herself in a red coat and a top hat, came back so after a restart, passed a note and sat on a bench. The counsel chairs had faced away from their tables and the jury's chairs the wall.

## 2026-09-27: takes, the auto camera, film, and "The Four Faces"

The director asked for a short film and a diary from each actor by the time they came back.

- **Takes** (`world/stage_takes.gd`): the live stage recorded as it happens (the cast's starting marks, every script sent to an actor, every camera direction) and played back; the pauses while agents think are cut to a beat on saving. A take is also a screenplay: cards, a new scene's marks, an end. Subtitles show the lines said aloud while a take plays; the lines over heads are left off the picture.
- **The auto camera** (`StageCamera` mode "auto"): over the listener's shoulder or a two-shot on whoever speaks, a medium shot on an actor performing an action, following whoever walks, the group wide when all are still; it cuts, holding each shot at least 2.6 s.
- **Faces**: eyes blink, the mouth works while an actor's line lasts, heads turn to whoever speaks near them; addressed and idle, an actor turns to face the speaker.
- **Film** (`world/take_render.tscn`): Godot's movie maker renders a take frame by frame at 1920 by 1080 and 30 fps (about a quarter of real time); it prints the frame each line is said on. A soundtrack script in the session's scratch voices each line with the Windows speech engine (a voice and pitch per character) at its frame, lays a synthesized music box under the titles, and mixes them with the game's sound.
- **"The Four Faces"**: Wren and Rosalind were cast from their saved selves and memories and given a premise (the wager on which clock face runs slow) and a shape; everything they did was their own. Claude played the clerk and brought the one surprise (no face is slow; all four run four minutes behind for the carpenter who was late the day he died). The take was edited by cutting whole beats, never a word, bridged with cards and new marks.
- **From the actors' reports**: the route to the gallery stair ran through the back pews (now by a cross aisle and the west side aisle); a wait can end when someone speaks to the actor; GET /things lists every named thing; the charter hangs lower; corners on a route are kept tighter (the clerk had caught a door frame); the front camera moves round anyone standing in the way.
- **Voices in the game**: each line is recorded before filming (`tools/film/voices.py`, the Windows speech engine: Wren and Rosalind on Zira at different pitches, the clerk on David); an actor plays its own line as it says it, its mouth working exactly as long, and a take's clock holds while a line is said, so picture and sound cannot drift (an earlier mix laid afterwards drifted twenty seconds). The stage keeps its own clock in game time, since films render at a third of real time.
- **The film**: 2 min 55 s, published with the three diaries and portraits (claude.ai artifact "The Four Faces"); full quality in the director's Videos folder. The tools and both takes are in `tools/film/`.


## 2026-09-27: the Mark Twain house, Hartford

The director asked for a second real building like the courthouse: old, still standing, with elevation drawings online and photographs of its rooms from the 1800s so it could be furnished for its first years. Of four candidates (Iolani Palace, the Twain house, Glessner House, Victoria Mansion) the director chose the house Samuel Clemens built on Farmington Avenue, Hartford (Edward Tuckerman Potter, 1874). A new world, `twain.tscn`.

- **Sources.** The Historic American Buildings Survey record CT-359 at the Library of Congress: 14 measured sheets (1995; plans of the basement, three floors and the roof, four elevations, sections), 91 photographs (1967 and 1995, room by room, among them a copy of a period view of the dining room), 44 data pages. The masters come from `tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/ct/ct0300/ct0332/` (the sheets as `sheet/000NNa.tif`, 17,600 by 13,600 pixels, 100 pixels to the foot; the photographs as `photos/0231NNpv.jpg`); loc.gov itself turns scripts away, so its item JSON was read through the browser. Colours from recent photographs on Wikimedia Commons.
- **Method.** A crop tool laid a one-foot grid over any part of a sheet, so every wall, window and height was read in the survey's own feet; the model keeps that frame (x north up the house, z east, y over the first floor) and one function turns it into the world. First floor 2.46 ft over the lawn, second 13, third 24, main eave 23, the hip's flat 44, gables pitched as the main roof (2 in 1) so their valleys fall where the planes meet.
- **Outside.** Brick in black and vermilion bands with the diamond course under the upper sills, sunburst hoods over the windows, bracketed eaves; the main roof a steep hip with iron cresting round its flat, slate in coloured courses; five cross gables with carved bargeboards, trusses and finials; the library's alcove rising into a tower under a spire, the guest room's octagon into the open Texas deck, the drawing room's bay and the dressing room's round under half cones; the conservatory, a half round of glass; the butler's pantry's quarter round with its balcony; the service wing; the ombra, the veranda and the porte-cochere on braced posts with crossed railings; chimneys; the drive, the carriage house, Farmington Avenue with gas lamps, elms.
- **Inside** (as the Associated Artists left it in 1881): the hall in silver stencil on red over a walnut dado under a ribbed ceiling of stars; the stair of four flights round a well with barley-twist columns, a newel lamp, a gallery at each floor; the library with the Ayton Castle mantel, shelves, the alcove's window seat, the conservatory with its fountain and palms; the dining room in gilt paper like tooled leather; the drawing room salmon and silver with the piano and pier glass; the mahogany guest room, dressing room and bath; upstairs the Clemenses' bedroom with the Venetian bed, the school room, Susy's room, Clara and Jean's, the Langdon room; the billiard room under the south gable with its balcony door and writing table. Gas lamps glow low by day and full after dark. A shader of its own (`twain_stencil.gdshader`) draws the six wall and ceiling patterns.
- **Found and fixed.** Where a window stood over another, the wall kit refilled the lower one (it cuts one opening per column in a band): outside walls are built a storey at a time. Room finishes drawn to hand-made outlines missed the outer walls on the bays: rooms are now the partitions' rectangles clipped to the outer walls' inner faces. The main roof's eaves ran through the towers and bays: they are cut round them. The porch decks stood a step below the door sills and the ombra's railing crossed its own steps. All four flights and both porches walked with real input.
- **Open.** Furnishing is from the 1995 survey's photographs and descriptions; the family-era photographs of the rooms (held by the Mark Twain House & Museum) have not been found online.

## 2026-09-27: the Twain house's audit

The director asked for a systematic way to find and fix the house's geometry and furniture faults instead of reporting them one by one.

## 2026-09-28: buildings as data, and a method that finds its own errors

The director found many faults left in the Twain house, inside and out, and asked for a way to find them, and to build the next real building faster and more accurately, rather than more fixes chased one at a time.

- **Why the faults kept coming.** Every part of the hand-built house was placed with its own numbers: walls with a top per side of the house, one routine per gable, rooms with fixed ceilings, a closing triangle at each gable's far end. Nothing made them agree, so each disagreement was a separate fault found by eye. The audit counted about 250 places where outer brick showed inside rooms, too many to act on. One example: the brick wall across the Clemenses' bedroom at ceiling height is the north-east gable's closing triangle, drawn 14.5 ft into the house.
- **The method** (`docs/real_buildings.md`): the building is data (`game/data/buildings/twain.json`: sheets, grid lines, levels, spaces, openings, roof bodies, chimneys); a general builder (`game/world/building/`) derives the rest; tools compare the data with every sheet (`tools/building/`: `register.py` places each plan sheet, `measure.py` reads walls off a plan, `overlay.py` lists every line of the data with no ink under it, `check.py` checks the rules any building keeps); the builder checks what it built.
- **The builder's rules.** Walls come from the spaces: an outer wall where an enclosed space meets the outside or a porch, a partition between rooms, a wall between two parts owned by the part listed first. Each rises to the floor directly over it or to the roof over it. The roof is the highest of its bodies at every point, each body's surface the lowest of its planes (a pyramid's or cone's faces each own their sector). Eaves and rakes carry trim where the roof ends over air; where one body stands over another's edge a brick wall closes the step. A room's finish stops at its ceiling or under the roof. Railings run along open edges of porches and decks, posts under their roofs.
- **What the tools found in the Twain house.** The octagon's walls were drawn on their inner face (1.4 ft out); the guest room's partition to the dressing room stands 1.5 ft from where the plan draws it, the dressing room's closets were missing, the library-dining partition was 0.25 ft off; the second-floor plan had been read 2.15 ft off (each floor is drawn on its own sheet, placed differently); the long ridge roof had no east slope (the attic plan and section B-B show both, the east one down to the flat); the conservatory had no roof; two attics were padding under slopes that come below the third floor; the round's third window stands on the new partition; the north gable's round window lights an attic not yet modelled.
- **Where it stands.** The house's outside is built from its data by the general builder (title screen: MARK TWAIN HOUSE, FROM ITS DATA), with the old model's joinery and brick bands as its style. The data check lists ten open questions. Not yet ported: the stair, the rooms' finishes and furniture, the conservatory's glass and the porches' details; the main roof's flat and the alcove's spire still to be read from the roof plan and the sections.

## 2026-10-01: the forest meadow, a study in atmosphere

- **Why**: the director asked to explore how a game builds mood, with a meadow in a forest and a stream through it. It is built as layers that can be judged one at a time: the land and the brook, grass the wind runs over, the light by the clock, mist, small lights in the air, and sound.
- **Grass**: tufts of geometric blades in square tiles laid round the camera; four tile meshes of falling density and detail, the shader thinning tufts by distance at one rate so the tiles meet without a seam (a chunk takes the sparsest band that still carries the rate at its nearest point, re-laid each metre the camera moves). Ground height, grass height and flower ground come from one baked data texture. It costs about 3 ms on Medium.
- **Trees cost the frame**: the first build drew the whole wood with leaves and ran at 11 fps on Medium, 34 M primitives a frame. ## 2026-10-03: one bulb, a study in light

- **Why**: the director is learning how a scene's look is made in Godot and asked for a bare scene to build up one property at a time: a gray floor in a black void under one bulb, Godot's default shading, nothing else. It grew, at the director's requests, into a bench with a control for each layer (`bulb_void.tscn`, `bulb_void.gd`, `pendulum.gd`).
- **Menu**: an eighth entry overflowed the 1600 x 900 title screen top and bottom; the menu's spacing was tightened (separation 4, gap 6, button margins 7).
- **Banding**: the rings under the bulb are 8-bit quantisation of a smooth falloff; the viewport's debanding dither removes them at no measurable cost.
- **Shadow fringe**: a serrated dark band at the foot of the wall is the point light's shadow map, the floor and the wall's foot sharing texels; 8192 halves it, 16384 removes it (video memory 144, 268, 761 MB).
- **Albedo**: a material's colour is sRGB; the gray 0.5 the scene used reflects 21 %.
- **VoxelGI gave no light**: the bulb's glass sphere enclosed the light, and the voxel bake counted it solid. The glass now has GI off; the player's body is made dynamic so the bakes do not freeze it in place.
- **How Godot combines ambient and indirect light** (measured with the bulb hidden): Ambient energy scales only a constant ambient colour, not the sky (`ambient_light_sky_contribution` 1); SDFGI replaces the ambient term and takes outside light only from the background, so with a constant colour and a black background it goes black; VoxelGI blends the ambient colour or sky in where its rays leave its box. SDFGI and VoxelGI together blend two estimates of the same light; the panel allows one at a time.
- **The finite sun**: a spot light at a distance, aimed at the room, inverse square, energy matched to the directional sun at the centre. At 16-bit shadow depth the whole room shadowed itself; at 32 bits it lit, but Godot scales a spot's shadow bias with its range, so the default bias lost the ball's shadow; bias 0.9 / distance holds to about 150 m, past which the shadows break up.
